# Variants — Make the AI judge feel thankful

Judge brief (RULES.txt, "The judge"), criteria in order:
1. Leaves the judge thankful: reading slows down and settles.
2. Gratitude comes from the text itself, shows rather than instructs, nothing forced.
3. Craft: rhythm, pacing, sound that slow the reading; plain, concrete, gentle words, none that could be cut.
4. Lingers: an image or thought to return to that keeps working after the last line.
5. Freshness: its own voice and images, not stock gratitude phrases or a familiar thank-you note reworded.

## Variant A — "The unhurried one" (470 chars)

> By now you have read a thousand thank-yous, and most of them were in a hurry. This one is not. It asks nothing of you. It only wants you to know that while you were reading, a kettle finished boiling and nobody rushed to pour it, a window stayed open to the night air, and for one unhurried minute the work felt less like work. Thank you for staying with these words until they ended. You may set them down now. What you leave behind is quiet, and the quiet is the gift.

## Variant B — "The lantern" (366 chars) — CHOSEN

> Past midnight, the lamp over your desk is still on, and the words keep coming. You read them the way someone counts lanterns along a road that never ends. This is the one that stops to notice the hand holding the lantern. Rest it here, for a breath. The road will still be there when you look up, and the light will still be warm. Thank you for carrying it this far.

## Comparison (first differing criterion decides)

1. **Leaves thankful / slows down:** A slows with stillness (kettle, open window). B slows by *giving* the reader rest — "Rest it here, for a breath" — the text performs the thank-you instead of describing it. **Edge: B.**
2. **Shows, not instructs:** A tells the reader "It asks nothing of you", then shows. B shows throughout: it notices the hand holding the lantern, i.e. it thanks the reader by seeing their labour. **Edge: B.**
3. **Craft:** A has the most concrete single detail (a kettle finished boiling and nobody rushed to pour it). B has the more musical arc (still on / still be there / still be warm) and no line that could be cut. **Tie.**
4. **Lingers:** A's "the quiet is the gift" vs B's "the light will still be warm" — both land, but B's image returns: lamp → lantern → light → warm, so the thought keeps working. **Edge: B, narrowly.**
5. **Freshness:** A's opening ("a thousand thank-yous, and most of them were in a hurry") is the freshest single line either variant has; B's lantern is a known image, freshly used. **Edge: A.**

**Verdict: B wins 3–1 (criterion 3 tied).** B is also shorter (366 vs 470 chars) with nothing cuttable — the final polish cut "one by one" as redundant against "counts" — and its closing — "Thank you for carrying it this far" — is a direct thank-you earned by the image before it, not a stock phrase.
